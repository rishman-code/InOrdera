Restaurant staff costs in the UK (2026–27): what one member of staff really costs, and what changes in April 2027

By the InOrdera team · Last checked 26 November 2026 · 6 minute read

The short answer The National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over is £12.71 an hour now and rises to £13.21 on 1 April 2027. On top of wages, employers pay 15% National Insurance on earnings above £5,000 a year, at least 3% pension contributions, and 5.6 weeks' paid holiday. A part-time role covering 25 hours a week at the April 2027 rate costs a restaurant about £21,200 a year once those costs and holiday cover are included.

What you pay on top of wages

  • Employer National Insurance: 15% on each employee's earnings above £5,000 a year (£96 a week) in 2026–27.
  • Employment Allowance: up to £10,500 off your employer National Insurance bill each year if you're eligible. It stays at £10,500 for 2027–28.
  • Workplace pension: at least 3% employer contribution on earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 a year, for staff who are auto-enrolled.
  • Paid holiday: 5.6 weeks a year (28 days for a five-day week). You pay for the holiday and usually pay someone else to cover the shifts.

The Low Pay Commission says employers still name the rise in employer National Insurance as the cost with the biggest impact. In the same advice, it says hiring in retail and hospitality is particularly weak.

How to reduce restaurant staff costs without cutting service

  1. Match rotas to demand. Use your till data to staff up for the busy hours and down for the quiet ones.
  2. Take repetitive work off your team. Answering the phone and reading back orders ties up a person for hours every night. That job can be automated.
  3. Cross-train your staff. People who can work front of house, on the pass and on prep reduce the need for extra cover.
  4. Claim the Employment Allowance if you're eligible. It takes up to £10,500 off your employer National Insurance bill.
  5. Keep the staff you have. Every leaver costs you recruitment and training time, so a small pay rise or better rotas can cost less than hiring.
  6. Cut other costs at the same time. Commission on app orders often costs more than a part-time wage. See our guide to Deliveroo, Uber Eats and Just Eat commission.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum wage for restaurant staff from April 2027?

From 1 April 2027 the National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over is £13.21 an hour, up from £12.71. The rate for 18 to 20 year olds rises to £11.27, and the under-18 and apprentice rates rise to £8.31.

How much employer National Insurance does a restaurant pay?

In 2026–27 employers pay 15% National Insurance on each employee's earnings above £5,000 a year. Eligible businesses can reduce their bill by up to £10,500 a year with the Employment Allowance, which is unchanged for 2027–28.

How much does a part-time restaurant worker cost per year?

A worker aged 21 or over doing 25 hours a week at the April 2027 rate of £13.21 costs about £21,200 a year. That includes wages, cover for 5.6 weeks' holiday, 15% employer National Insurance above £5,000 and a 3% pension contribution, before any Employment Allowance.

Do restaurants have to pay staff pension contributions?

Yes, for staff who are auto-enrolled. Employers must pay at least 3% on earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 a year, as part of a total minimum of 8%.

How can a restaurant cut staff costs without cutting service?

Match rotas to busy and quiet hours, cross-train staff, claim the Employment Allowance, and automate repetitive tasks such as answering the phone and taking orders so your team can focus on cooking and serving.
